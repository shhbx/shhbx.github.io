Multichannel Speech Enhancement with Spatial-Aware Explicit Phase Estimation

Your Name, Others, et al.
Paper Code

Abstract

Existing neural-network-based multichannel speech enhancement methods often rely on magnitude or real-imaginary modeling, but pay insufficient attention to explicit phase reconstruction and the effective exploitation of the spatial information and inherent geometric diversity of microphone arrays, which limits overall enhancement performance. To address these issues, this paper propose a novel Multichannel Speech Enhancement model with Spatial-Aware explicit Phase Estimation (SAPE-MSE). The SAPE-MSE adopts a magnitude-phase encoder-decoder architecture. The encoder encodes the multichannel degraded magnitude and phase spectra, followed by deep processing with improved time-frequency Transformers. Parallel decoders then estimate the clean magnitude and phase spectra at the reference channel, followed by inverse transformation to reconstruct the clean speech. Importantly, the phase decoding is spatial-aware, guided by spatial cues from inter-channel phase difference features, and explicitly estimates the clean phase spectrum via circular array spatial processing and phase parallel estimation. Experimental results demonstrate that SAPE-MSE outperforms representative multichannel speech enhancement methods on various metrics, achieving a PESQ score of 3.58 on a synthetic eight-channel dataset.
